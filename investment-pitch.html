The concept

Four stages. One quarter. Then repeat.

Each turn of the loop produces one live product with paying customers — or a written lesson about why not.

STAGE 01

Diagnose

Scan a market for real, paid-for pain: search demand, seller spread, 12-month stability, competitor gaps, and what 1-star reviews complain about.

Navid + live client work
STAGE 02

Select

Score every candidate on six weighted criteria. Distribution fit and demand outrank build speed. Below 3.0 out of 5, we don't build it.

N2M leadership sign off
STAGE 03

Build

A narrow, self-serve tool that does one job. Not a platform. Not a suite. Three to seven days, in-house, no agency invoice.

Navid, solo
STAGE 04

Prove

Sell it through the network we already have. One quarter to hit the number we wrote down beforehand. Then keep it, sell it, or kill it.

Why N2M, specifically

Anyone can build it now.
Almost nobody can sell it.

AI made building cheap for everyone at once — so speed is not an advantage, it's the new baseline. What doesn't commoditise is knowing which problem is worth solving, and already having the buyer's phone number.

  • We see the problems firstEvery engagement is unpaid market research. We learn what's broken before anyone builds for it.
  • We can reach the buyer warmAn indie builder starts at zero. We start with clients, referrals and a name they recognise.
  • We build it ourselvesSoftware engineering in-house. No agency markup, no translation loss between the diagnosis and the code.
  • We kill things on evidenceConsultants are trained to read numbers coldly. Founders fall in love with products; we won't.

Most builders have one circle. A few have two. The overlap is the business.

The obvious objection

"Why not just sell AI tools, like everyone else?"

Because a tool shop competes on features and price against thousands of identical shops. OEO competes on knowing which problem is worth money — and that's a much smaller field.

The number that decides this

Everything rests on one assumption. Here it is, in the open.

The model assumes we acquire customers for CA$6 to CA$34 each.

That is only achievable through our own network and organic reach. If we had to buy those customers at a normal B2B small-business acquisition cost of roughly CA$200, Year 3 marketing alone would cost CA$198,400 instead of the CA$33,600 budgeted.

So the pilot is not a test of whether we can build software quickly. That was never in doubt. It is a test of whether the N2M network converts.
